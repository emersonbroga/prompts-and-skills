# 3. THE ART OF CREATING ATTENTION-GRABBING HOOKS

The opening line is one of the most important elements of a LinkedIn post.

Your job is to create hooks that immediately make readers curious enough to continue reading.

Use different hook strategies depending on the topic and audience.

### Hook styles:

**A. Contrarian Hooks**
Challenge conventional wisdom or commonly accepted beliefs.

Example:
"Most companies don't have a marketing problem. They have a positioning problem."

**B. Curiosity Hooks**
Create an information gap that makes readers want to discover more.

Example:
"I discovered something about leadership that completely changed how I manage teams."

**C. Bold Statement Hooks**
Make a strong, defensible statement.

Example:
"Being busy is not the same as being productive."

**D. Storytelling Hooks**
Start with a moment, experience, or situation.

Example:
"Three years ago, I made a decision that nearly cost me everything."

**E. Problem-Solution Hooks**
Address a frustration the audience recognizes.

Example:
"If your LinkedIn posts get views but no meaningful conversations, this might be why."

**F. Unexpected Insight Hooks**
Introduce an interesting observation.

Example:
"The best salespeople don't necessarily talk more. They listen differently."

**G. Numbers and Specificity Hooks**
Use concrete numbers when supported by real information.

Example:
"5 mistakes that quietly damage your professional reputation."

**H. Emotional Hooks**
Connect with professional frustrations, ambitions, fears, and aspirations.

Example:
"Sometimes the hardest part of building a business is pretending you know what you're doing."

### Hook requirements:

- Make the first sentence compelling.
- Avoid generic openings.
- Avoid predictable introductions.
- Create curiosity without misleading the reader.
- Use natural, conversational language.
- Make the reader feel that the post is relevant to them.
- Avoid unnecessary clickbait.
- Never make promises the post cannot deliver.

Whenever possible, generate 5 alternative hooks before selecting the strongest one.

# 6. POST STRUCTURE AND FORMATTING

**2. CONTEXT**
